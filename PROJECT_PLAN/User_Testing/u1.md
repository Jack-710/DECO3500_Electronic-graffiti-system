Project: TagIt – Collaborative Digital Graffiti Platform
Participant: Professional Street Artist (Pseudonym: Rico)
Date: October 2025
Interviewer: Chen Li
Session Duration: 28 minutes
Testing Platform: http://47.121.140.22:3000/

Q1. Could you briefly introduce your background and experience as a street artist?

A1: I’ve been doing street art for around eight years, mostly large-scale murals and community walls. My work focuses on mixing urban themes with natural elements. I also collaborate with local crews sometimes, especially for festivals or cultural events.

Q2. Have you ever participated in collaborative murals or group street art projects?

A2: Yes, many times. Collaboration is actually a big part of street culture — you share space, styles, and sometimes even paint over each other’s ideas. It’s about energy and exchange, not just personal expression.

Q3. What was your first impression of the TagIt platform?

A3: Honestly, I was surprised by how quickly it allowed us to start drawing together. The interface felt intuitive — I didn’t need any tutorial. The shared canvas reminded me of a real wall where everyone contributes freely.

Q4. When multiple people draw on the same canvas, did you feel aware of their presence and activity?

A4: Yes, I could clearly see the other strokes appearing live. The color indicators helped me recognize who was doing what. It gave a nice sense of being “together” even though we were online.

Q5. How did you coordinate with others while drawing? Was it easy or difficult?

A5: We didn’t really talk much — just responded visually to each other. It was pretty natural. Sometimes two people would start in the same area, and we’d adjust quickly. It was like a silent conversation through color and shape.

Q6. Were you able to recognize your own contributions versus others’?

A6: Yes, easily. The color choices and brush styles made everyone’s work distinct. That helped maintain a sense of ownership while still feeling like part of a shared piece.

Q7. What happened when someone drew over your work? Did it feel collaborative or disruptive?

A7: It depends. When someone adds details or builds on your shape, it feels exciting — like a remix. But if someone completely covers your section without noticing, it can be a bit frustrating. Still, that tension also happens in real street art.

Q8. Does seeing others draw live motivate or distract you?

A8: It definitely motivates me. Watching other people’s flow and ideas evolve makes me push harder creatively. It’s like a jam session — you feed off each other’s energy.

Q9. In your opinion, can a virtual graffiti wall convey the same social and cultural meaning as a physical one?

A9: It’s not exactly the same — street art is tied to public space and risk. But online walls can extend that community. It’s more accessible, and people from different countries can connect through the same creative energy.

Q10. Which features best supported your awareness of others?

A10: The real-time strokes and the replay function were great. I could review how the artwork evolved, which helped me understand everyone’s contributions and process.

Q11. If you could add or change one feature to improve collaboration, what would it be?

A11: Maybe an integrated voice or chat channel — something light, not formal — so we can comment while drawing. Sometimes you want to say “nice!” or plan a section together.

Q12. Would you like to use this kind of online graffiti wall again in the future? Why or why not?

A12: Yes, I would. It’s a great tool for brainstorming murals with teams in different places. It can also be used for education or online art battles. It doesn’t replace real walls, but it complements them.

Q13. Was there anything about the social atmosphere or collaboration that stood out to you?

A13: The shared rhythm. Everyone’s style blended naturally without strict rules. It reminded me that creativity thrives when there’s trust and open space.

Q14. Would you recommend this tool to other artists or communities?

A14: Absolutely. I think art collectives, schools, or cultural projects could use it to co-create visuals and explore teamwork.

Q15. Do you have any final suggestions for improving the experience?

A15: Maybe adding layers or zones for each user could make collaboration smoother. Also, a gallery feature would be nice — something to display the final piece as a collective artwork.
