Interview 2 (Interviewee B)

Speaker 1:
What are your impressions of graffiti or interactive art in public spaces? Do they leave you with positive or negative feelings?

Speaker 2:
In my opinion, public graffiti is a creative form of expression that often inspires artistic expression, so overall, I think it's positive.

Speaker 1:
If there were a digital graffiti wall where you could collaborate with others, would you be willing to participate?

Speaker 2:
If it were completely public, I probably wouldn't be too keen. But if I could have a small, private area where others couldn't modify it at will, I'd be more willing. I don't like others directly re-creating my work.

Speaker 1:
Do you think activities like "Public Storytelling" would be appealing?

Speaker 2:
This format is quite interesting, similar to the "An Ke" (An Ke) format in running groups. Its advantages are that it's full of drama and unexpectedness, which encourages participation. However, if someone goes off topic or their writing doesn't fit the context, it can seem a bit awkward.

Speaker 1:
Would you be interested in interactive projections in the square that could be participated in from both near and far?

Speaker 2:
From a visual perspective, large-screen projections certainly attract attention and make people want to see them. However, I hope that in public spaces, they don't interfere with traffic or obstruct pedestrian vision, and ideally, they should be safe.

Speaker 1:
We also have an idea for "sound-to-painting," where drawings are generated based on the pitch and rhythm of sound. Do you think it's interesting?

Speaker 2:
I think it's great. It lowers the barrier to entry and allows even those who can't draw to participate. For those who are interested in art but lack the skills, this approach is user-friendly and a good experiment.

Speaker 1:
If you had to choose one of these approaches, which one would you be more interested in?

Speaker 2:
I'd probably lean towards "public storytelling." While I personally prefer private graffiti, from a public project perspective, storytelling allows for a better way to share and exchange ideas.

Speaker 1:
Okay, thank you very much for sharing!
