Interview 1 (Interviewee A)

Speaker 1:
What are your impressions of graffiti or interactive art in public spaces? Do you find them to be positive or negative?

Speaker 2:
I think graffiti is a very trendy and avant-garde hobby, with a strong street style and individuality. Overall, my impression is positive. I've heard there's an unwritten rule in the graffiti community: you can only cover someone else's work if yours is better than the original; otherwise, you can't just vandalize someone else's graffiti.

Speaker 1:
If there were a digital graffiti wall where you could collaborate with others, would you be interested?

Speaker 2:
No, because I'm not a good artist and I'm afraid of making a fool of myself.

Speaker 1:
What about a public storytelling activity where everyone takes turns writing stories? Would this interactive format appeal to you?

Speaker 2:
Yes, I think it's quite interesting. Everyone has their own creativity, and they can always come up with something interesting.

Speaker 1:
If there was a large interactive projection screen in the square, allowing people to participate remotely, would you be interested?

Speaker 2:
I probably wouldn't actively participate, but I might choose to watch from the side.

Speaker 1:
What about a "sound-to-painting" format, where the highs and lows of sound create a painting? Would you try it?

Speaker 2:
Yes, I think it's quite interesting. Even a passing dog could "participate," which would be very interesting.
