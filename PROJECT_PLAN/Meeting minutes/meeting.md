Agenda

Discussion of Problem Space in public art and graffiti.

Identification of Design Opportunities.

Key challenge: content regulation vs. instant creative freedom.

Exploration of potential solutions and next steps.

Discussion Summary
1. Problem Space

Traditional graffiti is often seen as vandalism and lacks legal expression channels.

High entry barriers: ordinary citizens have limited access to graffiti-like expression.

Current public installations lack real-time collaboration and social interaction.

Urban spaces lack mechanisms to encourage participation and co-creation.

2. Design Opportunities

Simulate graffiti digitally while avoiding legal issues.

Lower participation barriers through mobile devices or touchscreens.

Foster indirect dialogue between strangers via collaborative or competitive drawing.

Create a sense of community memory through works that accumulate and fade.

Gamify the public art experience with fading, overwriting, and voting mechanisms.

3. Key Challenge Identified

Moderation Dilemma:

Risk of inappropriate content (sensitive, discriminatory, offensive).

Strict censorship would conflict with instant creation and real-time display, which is central to the project’s vision.

4. Proposed Solutions Discussed

Introduce lightweight moderation mechanisms:

AI-based keyword/image filters for immediate screening.

Community flagging system where inappropriate works can be reported.

Consider layered visibility: potentially harmful drawings may be blurred or hidden temporarily until reviewed.

Balance creative freedom and public responsibility by:

Preserving spontaneity for most creations.

Limiting only clearly harmful or offensive content.

Design interaction rules that discourage misuse (e.g., works fade quickly, limiting the long-term impact of offensive drawings).

Decisions Made

Low-fidelity prototype will continue to be used for user interviews to collect feedback on interaction and moderation mechanisms.

Team will research AI-assisted moderation methods and community-based review systems.

Next design iteration will focus on balancing real-time creativity with safe public presentation.
