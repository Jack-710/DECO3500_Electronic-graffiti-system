🎤 Interview 1: Public Art Curator

Q1: When planning public art projects, how do you balance the freedom of artistic creation with the order of urban space?
C1: This is a common issue we face. Public art must balance freedom of expression with social responsibility. Traditional graffiti often sparks controversy due to its destructiveness and visual pollution, but digital methods can reduce this conflict. We hope to use new forms like digital graffiti to allow artists and the public to express themselves freely within a "legal creative space."

Q2: Do you think digital graffiti systems can address the urban management challenges posed by traditional graffiti?
C1: To some extent. Digital graffiti doesn't involve physical destruction and can be removed or updated at any time, making it more acceptable to governments. However, the key lies in the manageability of the technology—the system must be stable, secure, and monitorable. We also hope it retains a "street feel" rather than becoming a purely commercial display.

Q3: From your perspective, what are the most important considerations when designing this type of public digital art system?
C1: I think the most important ones are inclusiveness and the barrier to entry. Citizens don't necessarily have to be professional artists, so the system needs to be easy to use, even capable of voice or gesture control. Secondly, data and privacy protection are important, as public devices often collect user information.

Q4: Have you seen similar public digital art projects?
C1: Yes, for example, Melbourne's "Urban Screen Project," where citizens can upload graffiti videos using their phones in the square, and the system plays them back in real time. This participatory interaction is very popular, but backend maintenance and copyright management present the biggest challenges.

Q5: If Brisbane also has a digital graffiti wall in the future, what would you hope it would achieve?
C1: I hope it would become an open cultural interface—one that showcases art and inspires discussion. It would allow people to feel a sense of belonging to this city through their creations.
