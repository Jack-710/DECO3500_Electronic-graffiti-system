Project: TagIt – Collaborative Digital Graffiti Platform
Date: October 2025
Interviewer: Chen Li
Participants:
P1 – Mia (art student, experienced in digital painting)
P2 – Leo (freelance illustrator, occasional street artist)
Session Duration: 32 minutes
Platform Tested: http://47.121.140.22:3000/

Q1. How would you describe your first impression when using TagIt together?

P1: It felt really intuitive. We just opened the website and started drawing immediately. I liked that it didn’t require any setup or account. It gave a spontaneous vibe — similar to starting a graffiti wall on the street.
P2: Yeah, I agree. It felt light and open. The shared canvas looked simple, but once we began drawing, it was surprisingly interactive. You could instantly see what the other person was doing.

Q2. How did you coordinate with each other while creating?

P1: At first, we didn’t really plan. I started sketching something on the left, and Leo just began on the right. Then we sort of merged our drawings in the middle. It happened naturally.
P2: We didn’t need to talk much — we just watched each other’s strokes and responded visually. It’s like nonverbal communication through shapes and color.

Q3. Did you feel aware of each other’s actions during the process?

P1: Definitely. The live strokes made it easy to see when Leo was adding something. It’s like painting together in real time, not waiting for a refresh.
P2: The presence felt strong. You can feel that someone else is active there, even if you’re silent. It’s a kind of digital co-presence.

Q4. Did any overlap or conflict happen while drawing?

P1: (laughs) Yes, once. I accidentally drew over Leo’s line when I zoomed out. But it wasn’t a big deal — we both adjusted and turned it into part of the composition.
P2: Yeah, instead of erasing, we used the overlap to connect our drawings. It became a shared feature rather than a mistake.

Q5. Were you able to distinguish your own parts from the other’s work?

P1: Yes, I think the color choice helped a lot. I used warmer tones and Leo used cooler ones, so it was clear.
P2: I agree. Still, by the end it looked like one coherent artwork, which I liked — you could still see our styles, but it merged smoothly.

Q6. How did you feel about the social aspect — did it feel like collaborating or just drawing side by side?

P1: It definitely felt collaborative. I reacted to what Leo drew, and sometimes he added on top of my shapes. It was dynamic and playful.
P2: Same here. I think the platform creates a shared rhythm. You sense what the other is doing and adapt — like a visual conversation.

Q7. Did you communicate verbally or only through the drawing process?

P1: Mostly through drawing, but we also talked a little on Discord while using it.
P2: Yeah, it would be cool if TagIt had a small chat or voice option built in, so you could coordinate without switching platforms.

Q8. Did the platform support your creative flow or interrupt it?

P1: It supported it well. There was no lag, and I could focus on creating.
P2: I agree. It’s smooth. Maybe adding layer control would help for more complex collaborations, but the simplicity worked for this test.

Q9. How would you describe the feeling of co-creation online compared to physical graffiti or painting?

P1: It’s different, but still social. You miss the physical energy of being outdoors, but you get accessibility — anyone can join from anywhere.
P2: Yeah, the spirit of collaboration is still there. It’s like global graffiti — same creative energy, less limitation.

Q10. What feature stood out most to you?

P1: The live stroke feedback — seeing lines appear in real time.
P2: For me, it was the replay history. Watching how the wall evolved was fascinating. It showed the process and collaboration clearly.

Q11. If you could improve one thing, what would it be?

P1: Maybe add a small chat bubble system — just to say things like “let’s blend this” or “try that shape.”
P2: I’d like to see a way to highlight contributions or tag people’s areas, so you can analyze collaboration after finishing.

Q12. Would you use or recommend TagIt again?

P1: Yes, definitely. I think it’s great for online workshops or group brainstorming.
P2: Same. It’s simple but powerful — feels like a digital version of collective wall art.

Q13. Any final thoughts on the social or collaborative experience?

P1: I think the main strength is how it lets you feel connected without talking too much. It’s very “flow-based.”
P2: I agree. It keeps the balance between personal style and teamwork — that’s rare in creative tools.
