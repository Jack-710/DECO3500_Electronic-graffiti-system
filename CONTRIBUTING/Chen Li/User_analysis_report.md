User Analysis of the TagIt Collaborative Drawing Platform

The user testing session explored how two non-expert users experienced creativity and cooperation while using the TagIt collaborative graffiti platform. 
The pair—Tang, a craft hobbyist, and Li, a university student without formal artistic background—tested the web app together for thirty minutes on both laptop and mobile devices. 
They were encouraged to draw freely and interact naturally.

Both participants found TagIt highly intuitive. The interface required no instruction, allowing them to begin drawing immediately. This low barrier to entry made Li, 
who described himself as having “no art talent,” feel comfortable and confident. Tang commented that the experience was “spontaneous, like doodling together on paper,” 
highlighting the system’s accessibility for casual users.

The session generated strong evidence of social engagement. The couple coordinated smoothly without explicit planning, often responding visually to each other’s strokes. 
This form of nonverbal communication created what they described as a “shared rhythm” of creation. The platform successfully supported a sense of digital co-presence, 
enabling users to feel connected even through simple actions.

However, several usability issues disrupted the creative flow. Both users emphasized that the single brush option limited their artistic expression. 
They also noted the absence of undo/redo and zoom functions—essential features in most creative tools. The lack of an undo option particularly broke immersion when small mistakes occurred, 
forcing manual erasing. During mobile testing, Tang encountered a layout bug where a long filename on the save page concealed the delete button, preventing proper editing.

Despite these problems, participants described the overall experience as enjoyable and emotionally engaging. They valued TagIt’s social and playful nature, 
viewing it as an ideal tool for couples or friends who want to “do something creative together.” Both indicated that they would use the platform again if more drawing tools and responsive mobile features were added.

In summary, TagIt effectively supports collaborative creativity and emotional connection, even among users without artistic expertise. 
Yet to maintain engagement and accommodate diverse creative styles, the platform requires key improvements—expanded brush options, undo/redo and zoom functionality, 
and better mobile responsiveness. With these refinements, TagIt could evolve into an inclusive and socially rich environment that encourages creativity, communication, 
and shared expression in digital art.
